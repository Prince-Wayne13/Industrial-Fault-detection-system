## Roles (examiner question 5)

**Why three roles:** the system gives three different sets of rights: watching and acknowledging faults (Operator), fixing faults and tuning limits (Engineer), and managing the system (Administrator).

| What they can do | Operator | Engineer | Administrator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| View status, readings and fault history | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| See fault pop-ups | Yes | Yes | No |
| Acknowledge a fault | Yes | Yes | No |
| Mark a fault resolved | No | Yes | No |
| Put equipment in maintenance mode | No | Yes | No |
| Change limits | No | Yes | No |
| Generate reports | Yes | Yes | No |
| Manage accounts, equipment, settings and storage | No | No | Yes |
| Receive the 75% storage alert | No | No | Yes |

- **Operator:** the first responder on shift. Watches the dashboard, sees alerts, acknowledges them, generates reports.
- **Engineer:** diagnoses and fixes, often remotely. Does everything the Operator does, and also resolves faults, uses maintenance mode and changes limits.
- **Administrator:** manages the system. Gets no fault pop-ups and does not acknowledge faults.

The Management / Supervisor role is dropped. There is no "exactly one role" rule.

## Question 1: How is anyone told of an alert, and what does acknowledging do?

- **FR-7a:** When the status model confirms a fault, the system shall show a pop-up alert to every connected Operator and Engineer, whether near-range or remote.
- **FR-7b:** The pop-up shall show the equipment, the parameter, the fault type, the severity and the time.
- **FR-7c:** The system shall not send fault alerts to the Administrator.
- **FR-7d:** If no Operator or Engineer is connected, the fault shall stay open and be shown to the next Operator or Engineer who connects.
- **FR-8a:** An Operator or Engineer shall be able to acknowledge an open fault from its alert.
- **FR-8b:** When a fault is acknowledged, the system shall record which user did it and when.
- **FR-8c:** Acknowledging shall not close the fault. It stays open until an Engineer marks it resolved (FR-17). *Provisional: open item O-9 still waits for a formal yes.*

## Question 2: Can a limit change without rebuilding the system?

- **FR-29a:** An Engineer shall be able to change the warning limit and the critical limit for any parameter of any equipment type, from the dashboard, without restarting or rebuilding the system.
- **FR-29b:** The system shall reject a new limit if the warning limit is not below the critical limit.
- **FR-29c:** A changed limit shall apply to readings received after the change.
- **FR-23 (kept):** The system shall keep the old limit and the date it changed, and log who changed it.
- **FR-29d:** Each fault record shall store the limit that was in force when the fault was raised.

## Question 3: Security, and who acknowledged a critical fault

The rules are requirements. The methods (hashing tool, TLS setup) go in Appendix B as suggestions.

- **FR-26 (reworded):** A user session shall expire after 5 minutes of inactivity. A user may set a shorter time, but not shorter than 1 minute and not longer than 5 minutes.
- **Passwords 1:** Passwords shall be at least 15 characters long, and the system shall accept passwords of 64 characters or more.
- **Passwords 2:** The system shall not require a mix of capitals, numbers or symbols, and shall not force password changes on a schedule.
- **Passwords 3:** The system shall refuse passwords that appear on a list of common or leaked passwords.
- **Failed logins:** After 5 failed login attempts on an account, the system should block login to that account for 15 minutes. ("Should": optional.)
- **Data in transit:** All communication between the dashboard and the server shall use HTTPS (TLS 1.2 or newer).
- **Stored passwords:** The system shall store passwords only in a salted, hashed form that cannot be read back.
- **Who acknowledged:** answered by FR-8b.

## Question 4: How often is a reading taken, and what does that mean for storage and the 1% loss?

- **Sampling (new):** The system shall take one reading per second for every monitored parameter on every machine.
- **FR-13 (reworded):** If no reading arrives for a parameter for 3 seconds, the system shall flag a sensor fault on the dashboard.
- **FR-9a:** For every 10-second window, the system shall store the status and its time stamp.
- **FR-9b:** The readings of a normal window shall be deleted after 24 hours. The readings of a warning or critical window shall be kept with their status.
- **NFR-10:** The system shall keep statuses and warning or critical records until storage use reaches 75%, and shall never delete them automatically.
- **NFR-6:** Over any 24-hour period, the system shall store at least 99% of the readings it receives. The local store records the same readings at the same time as the main store.
- **PR-005:** The system shall process at least one reading per second for every parameter on every machine, and at least 40 readings per second in total (10 machines × 4 parameters). If a machine has extra parameters, the system shall still keep up.
- **PR-006:** The system shall have room for at least 3,456,000 readings (one day at the minimum load), plus statuses and fault records.

**Where 3,456,000 comes from:** 10 machines × 4 parameters × 86,400 seconds in a day (24 × 60 × 60). Each extra parameter on 10 machines adds 864,000 a day.

- **Storage alert:** At 75% of storage used, the system shall send an alert to the Administrator only.

## Question 6: Why is the alert target acceptable?

- **PR-001:** The system shall update each machine's status at least every 10 seconds.
- **PR-001a:** When the status is critical, the system shall show the alert immediately after the status confirms it, and no later than 2 seconds afterwards.
- **PR-001b:** If an alert is shown more than 2 seconds after its status confirms the fault, the system shall still show the alert, write the delay to the log, and show an "alerts delayed" notice on the dashboard.

*Why this is acceptable: a late alert means part of the system is interrupted or slow. The fault is still recorded and stays open, and is shown to the next user who connects. The cost of lateness is that the equipment runs longer before anyone acts.*

**Test:** measure the gap between the time the status confirms a fault and the time the pop-up appears, both on the synchronized clock (FR-25). The gap shall be 2 seconds or less.

The 10-second status check exists to rule out a random reading. A single abnormal reading never raises an alert (FR-6).

## Question 7: Product or demonstration?

The product is the real system. The demonstration is a dashboard with simulated data and a fault-injection mode. One sentence says so in DC-006 and Section 5: *The system is demonstrated with simulated sensor data and a fault-injection mode, and all tests are run on that simulation.* FR-11 and NFR-5 are not rewritten.
